Privacy Policy

Apex for iPhone · Last updated 13 September 2026

Apex does not collect your data. There is no account, no analytics, no tracking, no advertising, and no third-party services. Your training history is stored on your iPhone and goes nowhere else unless you ask it to.

This page describes the version of Apex currently on the App Store. If a later version ever collects anything, this page is updated before that version is released, and the Changes section below says how you will know.

Who is responsible

Apex is made and published by Stanislav Sysoev, an individual developer, who is the data controller for the purposes of the UK and EU General Data Protection Regulation. Questions about this policy, or about privacy in Apex, go to apex@sysoev.org.

What Apex stores, and where

Everything you enter — your programs, exercises, sets, weights, reps, bodyweight entries and settings — is written to a database inside the app's own storage on your device. It is covered by your device passcode and by iPhone's standard app sandboxing. None of it is transmitted to the developer, and the current version of Apex makes no network requests of its own.

What Apex does not do

In the current version:

  • No account, no sign-up, no email address, no password.
  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting or usage measurement of any kind.
  • No advertising, no advertising identifiers, no tracking across apps or websites. Apex does not ask for App Tracking Transparency permission because it has nothing to track.
  • No third-party SDKs that collect data, and no data sold, rented or shared with anyone.

Two of these are commitments rather than descriptions, and they hold whatever Apex does later: your training data is never sold or rented, and it is never shared with anyone for advertising.

Apple Health

If you grant permission, Apex writes a workout record — its duration and an estimate of active energy burned — to Apple Health when you finish a session. This is optional, it is off until you allow it, and you can revoke it at any time in Settings → Privacy & Security → Health → Apex.

Apex only writes to Apple Health and never reads from it. It requests no read permissions at all, so it cannot see any health or fitness data your device holds. Health data written by Apex stays in Apple Health on your device under Apple's own protections; it is never sent to the developer and never used for marketing.

iCloud backup

Apex can back up your training data to your own iCloud account so you do not lose it when you change phones. This is turned off by default and only happens if you switch it on in Settings.

When it is on, Apex writes a file containing your training data to the private iCloud container belonging to this app, under your own Apple ID. It keeps at most seven, one per day, and deletes older ones automatically. The developer has no access to your iCloud account and cannot read these files. Apple stores them and acts as the processor, under Apple's Privacy Policy. You can turn the setting off at any time, and you can see and delete the files yourself in the Files app under iCloud Drive → Apex.

You can also export a backup file manually from Settings and send it wherever you choose. Once you share that file, where it goes is up to you.

Purchases and the free trial

Apex is sold through the App Store. Apple handles the entire transaction: the developer never sees your name, payment details or billing address, and receives only Apple's aggregated, anonymised sales reports. Purchases are governed by Apple's Privacy Policy and the Apple Standard End User Licence Agreement.

To make the free trial last 14 days per Apple ID rather than resetting each time the app is reinstalled, Apex reads one value from Apple's own signed record of your purchase: the date this Apple ID first obtained Apex. That date stays on your device and is used only to work out when the trial started. No other purchase information is read or stored.

Children

Apex is a strength-training tool intended for adults. It is not directed at children, and it collects no data from anyone, including children.

Your rights, and deleting your data

GDPR gives you the right to access, correct, export, erase and restrict the processing of your personal data, and to complain to your local supervisory authority. In Apex these rights have almost nothing to act on, because the developer currently holds no personal data about you at all — there is nothing on our side to hand over, correct or erase.

Your own copy is entirely under your control:

  • There is no account, so there is no account to delete.
  • Deleting Apex from your iPhone permanently removes everything it stored on the device.
  • iCloud backups are separate: delete them in the Files app under iCloud Drive → Apex, or switch the backup setting off before deleting the app.
  • Health data written by Apex remains in Apple Health and is deleted there, in the Health app under Browse → Activity → Workouts.
  • You can export your full training history to a file from Settings at any time.

Changes to this policy

If this policy changes, the revised version is published on this page with a new date above, and material changes are noted in the app's App Store release notes.

Apex may add analytics in a future version, to understand which features are used and to find crashes. If it does, all of the following happen before that version is released, not after: this page is updated to name exactly what is collected, why, who processes it and how long it is kept; the App Store privacy label is updated to match; and if the data is ever personal to you rather than aggregated, the app asks for your consent in-app and works normally if you decline. Apex will not begin collecting anything under a policy that still says it collects nothing.
